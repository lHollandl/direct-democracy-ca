## 1. Core Vision

- A direct democracy platform where citizens post problems, propose solutions, discuss, and vote — with the results actually going somewhere.
- **The core insight:** an extreme version of democracy would consume our lives with meetings, jury boards, and review boards. But if everyone helps out just a tiny little bit, and can do it easily on their phones, then everyone working together could solve our problems.
- The government should be the people, not a separate thing.Utilizing our current level of tech advancement (internet and smart phones) direct democracy is possible and the people could take more power. 
- The goal is a website that can run itself, through direct democracy. Updates, new features  would be decided by the users. The development team works for the users. Its like an employee owned business, but I guess a consumer owned business. 
- **Why people would participate:** How do you get someone to work with someone else for free? If working together was easier we could solve the common problems of all Americans and develop solutions that we all agree on. Fixing things that would benefit everyone. 
- We need to make it easier for the people not only to work together but also to apply pressure on their representatives. Actually using the power the people have over their representatives.

## 2. Content Model: Problems, Topics, Umbrella Questions

### Basic unit
- Citizens input their perceived problems with their city/county.
- **Every posted problem must include a minimum of one solution.** this may need reworking
- Discussion boards populate with all these problems and solutions.
- Problems are slightly structured to be more positive and thought-provoking.
- 

### Umbrella problem architecture
- Every umbrella question has an **AI solution** — a solution that takes into account all the different types of questions and submitted solutions, based on how common those solutions and problems are and the type of response they get.
- To participate in forming an AI solution, you click on it and see all the groups of the different **variants** of the umbrella problem.
- For each variant you can:
  - Vote it positively or negatively
  - Submit an amendment to it
  - Submit a solution to it
- If a variant problem starts to make up the majority of influence, it starts to dominate the umbrella question.

### Keywords
- Provide a list of keywords to pick from, plus the ability to make new ones. This creates a network of thoughts.
- People who use similar keywords and similar phrasing form clusters.

## 3. Voting System

### Structure
- Voting is one tab on the page.
- Voting happens roughly **once a month**, open for about a week.
- All the engagement from that month drives the topics of voting.
- Past months' topics should be just as important as the present month's.
- You have to log on to vote.
- Certain ideas and topics discussed on the website get escalated to a vote.

### Integrity
- **Voting integrity needs research.** (Open item.)
- Votes need to be documented in a trustful and transparent way.

### Vote types
- Standard up / down vote.
- Plus a **strong approval and strong disapproval** option (double up or down vote). maybe, idk if the double up or down will be used

### Voter input as data
- When people vote, they can add their comments, ideas, suggestions, concerns, or expertise.
- Those inputs are fed back into the system of data analysis that drives the topic.

## 4. Outputs & Real-World Action

- After voting finishes, results are summarized into **one PDF per user**.
- The summary shows the user's engagement as well.
- The website can email the results to their representatives.
- Emails for city council / mayor / local government are already shown, so people can easily forward it themselves.
- Sent to everyone on the newsletter.
- **Final output:** California proposition proposals generated from topics discussed on the website.
- If made into an app, it would change everything.

## 5. Feed & Recommendation Algorithm

### Governing philosophy
- The universe works with many competing rules and laws. The website should have a number of ruling and controlling equations.
- Rules that decide how and why you see posts involve the **network of keywords** and the **network of problem summaries**.
- Think in terms of: gravity vs. entropy, quantum, heat transfer, conservation of matter.
- Maybe a bounce between chaos and order, between hate and positivity, agreement and disagreement.

### Cluster mechanics
- Group people together into a cluster, then slowly pull them apart so they make their own clusters.
- You see more problems posted by people whose problems you like. You see more of the people whose solutions you like. This builds small communities.
- **Random shortcuts:** you and another random person swap their recommended problems from like-minded people, at random. The majority of problems you see come from like-minded people; some come from very unlike-minded people. This diversifies public opinion, builds a cluster, and builds connections to other clusters.
- The more good shortcuts the better. The random problems in your feed *are* the shortcuts.

## 6. Behavior, Norms & Anti-Toxicity

- **Follow-through obligation:** if you decide to interact with a topic, you're somewhat obligated to keep interacting with it. Cap how much involvement you can have at one time. This discourages people from posting a ton and never following up — incentivizing conversation rather than shitposting.
- **Tit-for-tat needs strengthening.** Negativity is like a virus: it attacks and converts you into itself. We need to fight back — when it attacks, we prevent it from converting us, the virus dies, and we develop an immunity.

## 7. Platform Self-Governance

- A special page where users make suggestions for ways the website can be improved.
- Other people interact with those suggestions. If a suggestion gets approved, the developers build it.
- Developers also propose updates and additions, which the people then approve and decide on.
- A **developer homepage** where people can contact us with questions, and where we explain how the website works and how we built it.
- Random people are selected for **juries** for human review.

## 9. AI Accountability (DeFi / Internet 3.0)

- Every entry in a DeFi internet 3.0 system would also show its **level of manipulation by an AI**.An AI score
- Hold AI accountable and liable for manipulation and fabrication.
- If the AI breaks rules, its **social score decreases** — causing it to get less data, because people won't interact with it.
- But it regularly gets chances to redeem itself from the public, so it can always continue to grow.
- If the AI maintains a high social score — and if usage decreases without the social score decreasing — the AI gets rewarded massively and given a new topic. Or it can create a new topic, but it must be voted on by the people.
